<div class="case-study-header">
  <p class="eyebrow">Published research · Phase-field modeling</p>
  <h2>Deformation-induced amorphization</h2>
</div>
<div class="case-study-body">
  <h3 class="case-kicker">The question</h3>
  <p>How can a mesoscale model represent the coupling between plastic deformation and amorphous-phase evolution under severe loading?</p>
  <h3 class="case-kicker">The approach</h3>
  <p>This study couples a phase-field description with finite-deformation elastoplasticity and introduces a deviatoric-stress-dependent transformation strain, allowing phase evolution and mechanical fields to evolve together.</p>
  <h3 class="case-kicker">The contribution</h3>
  <p>The model describes amorphous-phase nucleation and propagation and captures experimentally reported features including amorphous shear bands, grain-size effects, and surface amorphization.</p>
  <div class="case-links"><a class="text-link" href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijsolstr.2025.113691">DOI</a> <a class="text-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.19043">Preprint</a></div>
</div>
</section>

</div>

  <h3 class="case-kicker">The question</h3>
  <p>How can remeshing support crystal plasticity simulations and inverse parameter calibration under large deformation?</p>
  <h3 class="case-kicker">The approach</h3>
  <p>This collaborative work develops an adaptive, quasi-differentiable remeshing framework for crystal plasticity simulation.</p>
  <h3 class="case-kicker">The contribution</h3>
  <p>The framework connects large-deformation simulation with gradient-based calibration of constitutive parameters.</p>

<section id="dislocation-dynamics" class="case-study">
<div class="case-study-header">
  <p class="eyebrow">Public preprint · Numerical methods</p>
  <h2>Energy-based discrete dislocation dynamics</h2>
</div>
<div class="case-study-body">
  <h3 class="case-kicker">The question</h3>
  <p>Can the energy structure of discrete dislocation dynamics be used to reach stable dislocation states more efficiently?</p>
  <h3 class="case-kicker">The approach</h3>
  <p>The study uses a non-singular stress-field formulation to keep interaction energy finite, recasts two-dimensional discrete dislocation dynamics as an optimization problem, and examines energy-minimization methods.</p>
  <h3 class="case-kicker">The contribution</h3>
  <p>Numerical comparisons show how energy-based optimization can accelerate relaxation toward stable dislocation configurations.</p>
  <div class="case-links"><a class="text-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.15831">View on arXiv</a></div>
</div>
